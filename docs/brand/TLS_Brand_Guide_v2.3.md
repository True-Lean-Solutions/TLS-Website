## Purpose

This guide is designed so a marketer, designer, sales partner, writer, or AI system can understand what True Lean Solutions does, who it serves, how it delivers, how it looks, and how it should communicate without needing the company re-explained each time.

## Precedence Rules for AI and Content Creators

> 1\. Follow the explicit request or campaign brief, provided it does not conflict with the claims and accuracy rules in this guide.
>
> 2\. Protect the TLS brand boundary: TLS is a consultative technology execution partner. It helps clients solve the right problem, then builds the solution. Organization-wide executive alignment and transformation direction belong to Tech Transpire.
>
> 3\. Use the service hierarchy, ideal customer profile, buyer pains, and audience matrix to tailor the message.
>
> 4\. Use the approved voice, words, visual identity, colors, gradients, typography, and component language.
>
> 5\. Never invent client names, client results, certifications, partner statuses, awards, proprietary methodologies, technical capabilities, or metrics.
>
> 6\. When source facts are insufficient, write a strong message without manufacturing proof.

## Understand & Confirm: Exact Meaning

A client may know the kind of outcome they need without having a complete scope. For example, they may know they need a portal, an automated workflow, a connection between systems, or a better application experience, but they may not know the detailed requirements, constraints, acceptance criteria, dependencies, data rules, or expected outputs.

TLS can create those requirements, and where a request would not solve the underlying need, say so and propose the right solution. This is solution-level consulting tied to delivery, not organization-wide transformation diagnosis.

## 1. Custom Software Development

Build applications, tools, and platforms designed around the client's workflows, data, users, and growth needs.

- Web and enterprise applications.

- Internal workflow systems.

- Customer, partner, and vendor portals.

- SaaS platforms and customer-facing digital experiences.

- API development, integrations, and microservices.

- Legacy system modernization and phased migration.

- Cloud-native applications.

## 2. System Integration & Automation

Connect existing tools, platforms, and data sources so they exchange information reliably and reduce manual handoffs.

- Integration architecture and data-flow design.

- Native connectors and middleware-based integrations.

- Custom APIs and direct database integrations.

- Workflow automation and real-time data movement.

- End-to-end validation, production monitoring, and optimization.

## 3. Technology Implementation & Optimization

Implement, configure, enhance, and support the applications and platforms a business relies on.

- CRM and operational platform configuration.

- Application enhancements and workflow configuration.

- Backlog execution and technical delivery capacity.

- Platform implementation, migration, or enhancement.

- Application-layer support and post-go-live optimization.

## 4. Process Improvement & Automation

Take a defined or definable workflow that creates friction and turn it into a better operating process through redesign and practical execution.

- Workflow mapping tied to a specific execution need.

- Process redesign and handoff simplification.

- Automation, configuration, integration, or supporting technology.

- Implementation, adoption, measurement, and refinement.

## 5. IT Recruiting

A supporting TLS capability for critical technical hiring. It must not define the broader brand.

- Role definition and success criteria.

- Technical sourcing and screening.

- Curated shortlists and interview support.

- Contract, contract-to-hire, and permanent technical roles.

- Roles may include developers, cloud and DevOps engineers, architects, data professionals, technical project leaders, QA/automation, security/IAM, and technical business analysts.

**13 Imagery & Content Structure**

## The Connection System - TLS's Visual World

Tech Transpire owns a world: mountains, paths, summits. TLS needs the same - a scene an image model can picture, not a vocabulary list. Abstract terms like "structured grids" and "flow lines" describe any B2B software diagram ever made, which is why generic stock iconography fills the gap. TLS's world is already in the logo: separate things joined at a junction.

The grammar, applied to every TLS graphic:

- Something fragmented, manual, or scattered on one side.

- Something built, reliable, and whole on the other side.

- TLS's work is visibly the connection between them - the junction is the subject of the image, not decoration.

Color carries the direction (fixed - never reverse it):

- Execution Red \#D94040 - the current, messy, manual side. Where the problem lives.

- Bridge Plum \#873662 - the junction. The connection TLS builds. This is the hinge of the composition.

- Build Blue \#1288CF - the built, reliable, working side. Where it lands.

Red is not a sprinkled accent. It is structurally half the composition. A TLS graphic rendered predominantly in blue with a single red mark is off-brand: it reads as generic blue SaaS and collides visually with Tech Transpire.

Every pillar fits this grammar: integration (systems joining), custom software (scattered tools becoming one), process automation (manual steps becoming a flow), build-vs-buy (two routes from one junction), scoping (vague need becoming clear scope).

## Icons: Structural vs. Decorative

The test is function, not quantity. If deleting the icon leaves the viewer confused about what the object IS, keep it. If it just removes clip-art, cut it.

- Structural (unlimited) - the icon IS the thing. An envelope makes a card an inbox. A grid makes it a spreadsheet. A cylinder makes it a database. It carries meaning a viewer needs.

- Decorative (maximum 2, prefer zero) - the icon represents a concept. A shield for "secure", a lightbulb for "idea", a gear for "process", a handshake for "partnership", a checkmark badge for "quality". This is the stock-library reflex and it is what makes TLS graphics look like everyone else's.

Objects in a TLS graphic must be identifiable and specific - a real spreadsheet with visible rows, a real form with labelled fields, a real application with a navigation column. Not placeholder greyboxes, not flat concept icons standing in for real things.

## Brand Distinctness (hard)

A TLS graphic and a Tech Transpire graphic must never be mistakable for one another. They are separate brands and their visuals must be structurally incompatible.

- TLS: systems, horizontal flow, no human figures, ends at a working system. Red-plum-blue.

- Tech Transpire: aspirational, vertical ascent, may include a human figure, ends at a summit. Navy-blue only.

If a TLS graphic could carry the Tech Transpire logo without looking wrong, it has failed. Redesign it.

- Clean system diagrams and architecture blocks.

- Integration maps and data-flow lines.

- Workflow visuals and implementation boards.

- Product screens, dashboards, and real application interfaces when available and approved.

- Code-light technical visuals that suggest engineering without becoming a wall of code.

- Modern workspaces and hands-on delivery environments.

- Structured grids, nodes, connected systems, controlled movement, and operational flow.

- Isometric system visuals: glass tiles or panels around a central platform that carries the TLS symbol, with a restrained red-to-blue edge light, each panel labelled with a real system, problem, or outcome. Color direction follows the Connection System: red on the problem side, blue on the built side.

- Delivery roadmaps when they represent execution steps rather than transformation strategy.

## Visuals to Avoid

- Mountains, paths, and arrows used as broad strategic-journey metaphors - those are closer to Tech Transpire.

- Executive-only boardroom imagery and abstract leadership visuals.

- Stock handshakes, call centers, generic smiling office teams.

- Random code walls, binary code, cyber/SOC imagery, hackers, hooded figures.
